## Chapter 1: Present Tenses

### 1.1 Simple Present

**Form:** base verb (+ s/es for he/she/it)

**Use:**
- Scientific facts and universal truths: *Water boils at 100 degrees.*
- Permanent situations: *Julie lives in London.*
- Habits and regular actions: *I drink coffee every morning.*
- Future events on timetables: *My plane leaves at eight tonight.*
- Future meaning after 'when', 'until', 'after', 'before', 'as soon as': *I'll call you when I get home.*
- To talk about books, plays, and films: *At the end of the book, the detective catches the killer.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + V1 + s/es + Object | She writes a letter. |
| Negative | Subject + does not + V1 + Object | She does not write a letter. |
| Question | Does + Subject + V1 + Object | Does she write a letter? |
| Negative Question | Does + Subject + not + V1 + Object | Does she not write a letter? |

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I working in London. | I work in London. | The -ing form is not used in the present simple. |
| He work in London. | He works in London. | Third person (he, she, it) adds the letter -s. |
| Work he in London? | Does he work in London? | Questions: does + subject + infinitive. |
| He not work in London. | He doesn't work in London. | Negatives: subject + doesn't + infinitive. |

---

### 1.2 Present Continuous (Progressive)

**Form:** am/is/are + verb-ing

**Use:**
- Actions happening at the moment of speaking: *The water is boiling now.*
- Temporary situations: *Julie is living in Paris for a few months.*
- Situations that are slowly changing: *I'm getting better at speaking English.*
- Temporary or new habits: *I'm drinking too much coffee these days.*
- Annoying habits (with 'always'): *My flatmate is always leaving the kitchen in a mess!*
- Definite future plans: *I'm meeting John after class today.*
- To talk about people in pictures/photos: *In this photo, my mother is walking beside a lake.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + am/is/are + V1 + ing + Object | She is writing a letter. |
| Negative | Subject + am/is/are + not + V1 + ing + Object | She is not writing a letter. |
| Question | Am/Is/Are + Subject + V1 + ing + Object | Is she writing a letter? |

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| They still waiting for you. | They are still waiting for you. | To form a continuous tense, we use be + -ing. |
| Do they still waiting for you? | Are they still waiting for you? | In questions, the subject and auxiliary verb (be) change places. |
| She doesn't watching TV. | She isn't watching TV. | To form the negative, we put not after the verb be. |
| I'm believing you. | I believe you. | Some verbs (stative verbs: believe, come from, cost, exist, feel, hate, like) are not used in continuous tenses. |

**Present Simple vs Present Continuous:**
| Present Simple | Present Continuous |
| --- | --- |
| Things which are always true: *Water boils at 100 degrees.* | Things happening at the moment: *The water is boiling now.* |
| Permanent situations: *Julie lives in London.* | Temporary situations: *Julie is living in Paris for a few months.* |
| Habits: *I drink coffee every morning.* | Temporary/new habits: *I'm drinking too much coffee these days.* |
| Future timetables: *My plane leaves at eight tonight.* | Definite future plans: *I'm meeting John after class today.* |
| Future after 'when': *I'll call you when I get home.* | â€” |

### 1.3 Present Perfect

**Form:** have/has + past participle

**Use:**
- Actions that started in the past and have just finished: *She has written a letter.*
- Actions that started in the past and continue to the present: *I have been here since last week.*
- Life experiences: *Have you ever visited London?*
- Actions that happened at an unspecified time in the past: *He has lost his keys.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + have/has + V3 + Object | She has written a letter. |
| Negative | Subject + have/has + not + V3 + Object | She has not written a letter. |
| Question | Have/Has + Subject + V3 + Object | Has she written a letter? |

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steven has wrote a new book. | Steven has written a new book. | The past participle must be used â€” 'wrote' is past simple, 'written' is past participle. |
| Did you have seen him before? | Have you seen him before? | The helping verb 'have' is used in the present perfect. |
| I didn't have seen him before. | I haven't seen him before. | To make it negative, add not (n't) after 'have'. |
| I am here since last week. | I have been here since last week. | The present perfect shows an action which continues to the present. |
| I've been knowing him for 5 years. | I've known him for 5 years. | Verbs like know, want, like (stative verbs) suggest permanent states, not actions, so are used in the simple form. |

**Signal words:** just, yet, never, ever, already, so far, up to now, since, for, recently

### 1.4 Present Perfect Continuous (Progressive)

**Form:** have/has + been + verb-ing

**Use:**
- Actions that began in the past and have just stopped: *I have been studying English for two years.*
- How long the action has been happening: *She has been driving for twenty minutes.*
- Emphasis on the duration of an activity: *I have been working here for 5 years.*
- To show the cause of a present action: *Kim is tired because she has been working all day.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + have/has + been + V1 + ing + Object | She has been writing a letter. |
| Negative | Subject + have/has + not been + V1 + ing + Object | She has not been writing a letter. |
| Question | Have/Has + Subject + been + V1 + ing + Object | Has she been writing a letter? |

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| It has been rain heavily all day. | It has been raining heavily all day. | The structure is have/has been + -ing. |
| I have sat here for two hours. | I have been sitting here for two hours. | Non-stative verbs suggest continuity and are mostly used in the continuous form. |

**Signal words:** since, for, all day, how long

**Note:** When both the simple and continuous form are possible, native speakers prefer the continuous.

## Chapter 2: Past Tenses

### 2.1 Simple Past

**Form:** past form of the verb (V2)

**Use:**
- Actions completed in the past: *She wrote a letter.*
- Past habits: *I played football when I was a child.*
- Actions that happened at a specific time in the past: *I saw him yesterday.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + V2 + Object | She wrote a letter. |
| Negative | Subject + didn't + V1 + Object | She didn't write a letter. |
| Question | Did + Subject + V1 + Object | Did she write a letter? |
| Negative Question | Did + Subject + not + V1 + Object | Did she not write a letter? |

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I was work in London. | I worked in London. | In positive sentences, a helping verb such as 'was' or 'did' is not used. |
| Did he wrote a letter? | Did he write a letter? | The main verb is used in the infinitive form in questions and negatives. |
| He didn't wrote a letter. | He didn't write a letter. | The main verb is in the infinitive form after 'didn't'. |

**Signal words:** last ..., ... ago, in 1990, yesterday, regular verbs: infinitive + ed, irregular verbs: 2nd column of irregular verbs table

### 2.2 Past Continuous (Progressive)

**Form:** was/were + verb-ing

**Use:**

**USE 1 â€” Interrupted Action in the Past:** A longer action was interrupted by a shorter action.
*I was watching TV when she called.*
*While we were having the picnic, it started to rain.*

**USE 2 â€” Specific Time as an Interruption:**
*Last night at 6 PM, I was eating dinner.*
*At midnight, we were still driving through the desert.*

**USE 3 â€” Parallel Actions:** Both actions happening at the same time.
*I was studying while he was making dinner.*
*While Ellen was reading, Tim was watching television.*

**USE 4 â€” Atmosphere:** A series of parallel actions to describe a scene.
*When I walked into the office, several people were busily typing, some were talking on the phones, the boss was yelling directions, and customers were waiting to be helped.*

**USE 5 â€” Repetition and Irritation with 'always':**
*She was always coming to class late.*
*He was constantly talking. He annoyed everyone.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + was/were + V1 + ing + Object | She was writing a letter. |
| Negative | Subject + was/were + not + V1 + ing + Object | She was not writing a letter. |
| Question | Was/Were + Subject + V1 + ing + Object | Was she writing a letter? |

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I waiting for him almost two hours. | I was waiting for him almost two hours. | Past continuous uses was/were + -ing. |
| What did he doing when you saw him? | What was he doing when you saw him? | Use was/were in questions, not 'did'. |
| We were playing tennis every morning. | We played tennis every morning. | Use the past simple for repeated actions in the past. |
| They watched TV when I came. | They were watching TV when I came. | Past continuous shows what was happening at a particular time in the past. |

**While vs When:** "When" is usually followed by the simple past; "while" is usually followed by the past continuous.
*I was studying when she called.*
*While I was studying, she called.*

**Non-Continuous Verbs:** Non-continuous verbs cannot be used in any continuous tenses. Use simple past instead.
*Jane was at my house when you arrived.* (Correct)
*Jane was being at my house when you arrived.* (Not Correct)

**Signal words:** while

### 2.3 Past Perfect

**Form:** had + past participle

**Use:**

**USE 1 â€” Completed Action Before Something in the Past:**
*I had never seen such a beautiful beach before I went to Kauai.*
*I did not have any money because I had lost my wallet.*
*She only understood the movie because she had read the book.*

**USE 2 â€” Duration Before Something in the Past (Non-Continuous Verbs):**
*We had had that car for ten years before it broke down.*
*By the time Alex finished his studies, he had been in London for over eight years.*
*They felt bad about selling the house because they had owned it for more than forty years.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + had + V3 + Object | She had written a letter. |
| Negative | Subject + had + not + V3 + Object | She had not written a letter. |
| Question | Had + Subject + V3 + Object | Had she written a letter? |

**Past Perfect vs Simple Past:**
When "before" or "after" is used, the order of events is clear, so the past perfect is optional:
*She had visited her relatives once in 1999 before she moved in with them in 2001.*
*She visited her relatives once in 1999 before she moved in with them in 2001.*

However, if the past perfect is not referring to an action at a specific time, it is NOT optional:
*She never saw a bear before she moved to Alaska.* (Not Correct)
*She had never seen a bear before she moved to Alaska.* (Correct)

**Past Perfect in Time Clauses:**
After "when": both tenses possible, but with different meanings.
*When the film ended he switched off the television.* (actions closely connected)
*When she had washed the dishes she had a cup of tea.* (first action completed before second started)

After "after": past perfect is much more usual.
*After Zidane had scored the goal the fans went wild.*

Also used with: as soon as, until, before, by the time.
*He got up as soon as he had heard the alarm clock.*
*We did not stop until we had reached the coast.*

**Specific Times with Past Perfect:** Unlike the present perfect, specific time words can be used with the past perfect.
*She had visited her Japanese relatives once in 1993 before she moved in with them in 1996.*

**Signal words:** already, just, never

---

### 2.4 Past Perfect Continuous (Progressive)

**Form:** had been + verb-ing

**Use:**
- Activities that began before a point of time in the past and were still continuing at that point:
*Last summer Josh had been renovating his house for two years.*
(He started three years ago and last summer he was still renovating.)
- How long something had been happening before something else happened:
*I had been learning English in this school for 20 days.*
- To show the cause of a past action:
*Kim had a car accident because she had been driving for 12 hours non-stop.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + had been + V1 + ing + Object | She had been writing a letter. |
| Negative | Subject + had + not been + V1 + ing + Object | She had not been writing a letter. |
| Question | Had + Subject + been + V1 + ing + Object | Had she been writing a letter? |

**Past Perfect Simple vs Continuous:**
For actions that can continue for a long time (work, run, study, travel, sleep), both forms are possible with practically no difference in meaning, but the continuous form is more usual:
*Stephen was pretty tired. He had worked all day.*
*Stephen was pretty tired. He had been working all day.*

In other cases, they have completely different meanings:
*Before midnight Paul had translated the article.* (He finished his work.)
*Before midnight Paul had been translating the article.* (He did not finish it. He was still translating at that moment.)

For a number of individual actions or repeated actions, use the past perfect simple:
*Before the lesson ended they had written three tests.* (three individual completed activities)
*It was exhausting. They had been writing tests since the lessons started.* (one uninterrupted incomplete activity)

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I had working hard, so I felt very tired. | I had been working hard, so I felt very tired. | The form is had + been + verb-ing. |
| I had been heard the song many times before. | I had heard the song many times before. | Some verbs (stative verbs: know, like, understand, believe, hear) are not normally used in the continuous form. |

**Signal words:** how long, since, for

## Chapter 3: Future Tenses

### 3.1 Simple Future

**Form:** will + base verb

**Use:**
- Predictions about the future (you think something will happen): *I think it will rain tomorrow.*
- Spontaneous decisions made at the time of speaking: *The phone's ringing. OK, I'll answer it.*
- Promises: *I promise I'll help you.*
- Main clause in type I conditional sentences: *If you give me your address, I'll send you a postcard.*
- Expressing what you think or expect: *I'm sure he will help you.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + will + V1 + Object | She will write a letter. |
| Negative | Subject + will + not + V1 + Object | She will not write a letter. |
| Question | Will + Subject + V1 + Object | Will she write a letter? |

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I'll call you when I'll come to my office. | I'll call you when I come to my office. | In adverbial clauses, use present simple after when, as soon as, until. |
| If you will give me your address, I'll send you a postcard. | If you give me your address, I'll send you a postcard. | In conditional clauses, use the present simple. |
| I can't see you next week. I will return to Paris. | I can't see you next week. I am returning to Paris. | To show the decision was made in the past, use present continuous or 'going to' future. |

---

### 3.2 'Going To' Future

**Form:** am/is/are + going to + base verb

**Use:**
- Actions that have been decided before the time of speaking: *I am going to buy a new car.*
- When we see (and are sure) that something will happen: *Look! You are going to drop your books.*
- What you think will happen: *I think it's going to rain.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + am/is/are + going to + V1 + Object | She is going to take a holiday. |
| Negative | Subject + am/is/are + not + going to + V1 + Object | She is not going to take a holiday. |
| Question | Am/Is/Are + Subject + going to + V1 + Object | Is she going to take a holiday? |

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You know that I'll buy a new car, don't you? | You know that I am going to buy a new car, don't you? | We use 'going to' for a future action that has been decided before speaking. |
| Look! You will drop your books. | Look! You are going to drop your books. | We use 'going to' if we see (and are sure) that the action will happen. |

---

### 3.3 Future Continuous (Progressive)

**Form:** will be + verb-ing

**Use:**
- An action that will be in progress at a certain time in the future: *I'll be waiting there at five o'clock.*
- Something that happens because it normally happens: *This time tomorrow, I will be enjoying the cricket match in the stadium.*
- Planned or expected actions: *They will be staying for a week.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + will be + V1 + ing + Object | She will be writing a letter. |
| Negative | Subject + will + not be + V1 + ing + Object | She will not be writing a letter. |
| Question | Will + Subject + be + V1 + ing + Object | Will she be writing a letter? |

---

### 3.4 Future Perfect

**Form:** will have + past participle

**Use:**
- Something will already have happened before a certain time in the future:
*By the time we get there, the film will have started.*
*They will have shifted the house by Sunday morning.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + will have + V3 + Object | She will have written a letter. |
| Negative | Subject + will + not have + V3 + Object | She will not have written a letter. |
| Question | Will + Subject + have + V3 + Object | Will she have written a letter? |

---

### 3.5 Future Perfect Continuous (Progressive)

**Form:** will have been + verb-ing

**Use:**
- Something will already have happened before a certain time in the future, with emphasis on the duration:
*Next year, I will have been working in the company for 10 years.*

**Structure:**
| Form | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | Subject + will have been + V1 + ing + Object | She will have been writing a letter. |

### 4.2 First Conditional

**Form:** If + present simple, will + base verb

**Use:** For real and possible situations in the future.
*If I see him, I'll tell him.*
*If you don't hurry, you'll miss the bus.*

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |

| I'll tell him as soon as I will see him. | I'll tell him as soon as I see him. | We use present simple in the if-clause. |

---

### 4.3 Second Conditional

**Form:** If + past simple, would + base verb

**Use:** For unlikely or imaginary situations in the present or future.
*If I had more time, I would travel more.*
*If you didn't hurry so much, you would feel more relaxed.*

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| If I would have enough money, I would buy a new computer. | If I had enough money, I would buy a new computer. | We use past simple (I had) in the if-clause for unlikely/imaginary situations. |
| If you didn't hurry so much, you will feel more relaxed. | If you didn't hurry so much, you would feel more relaxed. | The main clause uses 'would + infinitive'. |

---

### 4.4 Third Conditional

**Form:** If + past perfect, would have + past participle

**Use:** For impossible situations in the past (things that did not happen).
*If I had known you were coming, I would have waited for you.*
*If she hadn't been ill, she would have gone to the cinema.*

**Common Mistakes:**
| Mistake | Correct | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| If I would have asked him, he would have helped me. | If I had asked him, he would have helped me. | In the if-clause, we use the past perfect (had + past participle). |
| If you had spoken to my mother, she would tell you where I was. | If you had spoken to my mother, she would have told you where I was. | The main clause uses 'would have + past participle'. |

**Form:** should + have + past participle

**Use:** To say someone didn't do something, but it would have been better to do it.

**Present:** *I must go.* / *I have to go.* (obligation)

**Past:** *I had to go.* (obligation)

### 5.6 Present Probability â€” Must / Can't / May / Might / Could

**Must (sure something is true):**
*They are really good, they must win.*
*They must be very rich. Look at the house.*

**Can't (sure something is impossible):**
*She can't be ill. I've just seen her in the shop.*

*He must have already gone.*

**Can't have / Couldn't have (sure something was impossible):**

**May have / Might have / Could have (something was possible):**
*He may have missed the bus.*
*The road might have been blocked.*

*The assistant might not have received his message.*

## Chapter 6: Verb Structures

### 6.1 Verb + Bare Infinitive

The bare infinitive (without 'to') is used after certain verbs followed by an object: let, make, see, hear, feel.
*My parents didn't let me watch TV at night.*
*Did you see anyone enter the building?*
*He made me laugh.*

The bare infinitive is used after modal verbs: can, should, could, might, may, etc.
*He can't sing.*
*It might be a good idea.*

### 6.2 Verb + -ing (Gerund)

The gerund form (-ing) is used after certain verbs: avoid, admit, can't stand, deny, dislike, enjoy, hate, like, love, mind, practise.
*I enjoy going to the cinema.*
*I don't like living in a city.*

The gerund is used after verb + preposition: insist on, look forward to, etc.
*I'm looking forward to seeing you.*
*He insisted on seeing the shop manager.*

### 6.3 Verb + To + Infinitive

The 'to' infinitive is used after verbs: agree, arrange, decide, offer, seem, plan, want, need, promise, hope, say, refuse, start, stop.
*I have decided to lend him the car.*
*He offered to help me.*
*He promised not to say anything.*

The 'to' infinitive is used after certain verbs followed by an object: ask, tell, want, need.
*He asked me to go there with him.*
*She told me to help her.*

### 6.4 Verb + Object + To-Infinitive

After some verbs, an object is included before the to-infinitive: advise, ask, allow, expect, encourage, force, help, invite, order, persuade, teach, tell.
*They considered him to be the best person for the position.*
*She asked me to give her some advice.*
*I told my assistant to send the letter.*
